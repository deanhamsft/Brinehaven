- Never whistle in a mine.  
    Whistling calls the Knockers (or worse, mimics their tapping). If they hear you, they’ll tap back… in the wrong places.
- Never start a new tunnel on a Friday.  
    Friday belongs to the dead. Begin work then and the first stone you break will be someone’s gravestone.
- Always leave a dram of ale or a heel of black bread at the tunnel face every shift.  
    Payment for the “Old Man of the Mountain” or the Tommyknockers. If you forget, the props start cracking by morning.
- If your lamp goes blue, snuff it at once and walk (never run) to the last crosscut.  
    Blue flame means firedamp or the breath of something older than fire.
- Never speak the name of the duergar underground.  
    Call them “the grey ones” or “deep cousins.” Say the real name and they’ll hear you through solid stone.
- A single falling drop of water that rings like a bell is the mountain weeping.  
    Stop work, reinforce everything, and get out before it starts screaming.
- Women in the mine are bad luck… unless they’re bearded dwarf-women.  
    A smooth-cheeked human woman means cave-in within the week. A bearded dwarrowdam carrying her own pick means the vein will run rich.
- If you find a pick left blade-up in the dark, do not touch it.  
    Someone died there. Taking it claims their death for your own.
- Never count the bodies after a collapse until they’re all above ground.  
    Count too soon and the mountain will adjust the tally.
- A cave cricket that sings inside the mine is good fortune.  
    Kill one and the tunnel seals forever.
- Always enter and leave by the same shaft mouth in one shift.  
    Using two doors confuses the mountain about whether you still belong to the surface world.
- If two miners’ shadows on the wall merge into one, one of them will not see daylight again.
- Never sleep with your boots on underground.  
    You’ll wake up walking deeper, following a voice that knows your true name.
- When the canary stops singing, you have until the count of ten heartbeats to decide who leaves first.  
    Argue longer and no one leaves.
- A vein of ore that shines red by lantern-light is dragon-tainted.  
    Take only what you can carry in one hand and seal the tunnel behind you. Greedier miners have been found years later, still digging, skin hardened to scales.
- The deepest superstition of all:  
    Never, ever dig straight down. There are things the roots of the world were meant to keep buried, and they hate the sound of picks.
